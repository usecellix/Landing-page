Credit System

Credit Cost Map — Recalibrated for 500 Credits

500 credits is the right number for the Solo plan. A moderate CA (4–6 GST reconciliations, 8–10 TDS checks, 15 formula fixes, 20 Q&As per month) uses ~390 credits — 78% utilisation. A heavy CA hits ~445 credits (89%). Both are comfortable. The ceiling case (filing season + audit work) might push to 520 credits, triggering a natural top-up purchase. This is intentional — the credit architecture creates a revenue event at exactly the right moment (peak CA stress = highest willingness to pay).

Typical Solo CA month uses 388 of 500 credits (78%): 5 GST reconciliations (110 cr) + 8 TDS checks (96 cr) + 15 formula fixes (120 cr) + 25 Q&As (50 cr) + 3 audit trail exports (12 cr) = 388 credits. Execution Strategy

Pricing Launch Cadence — The Full Playbook

This is the most important section. The pricing numbers are only half the decision. The other half is when to launch what, to whom, in what order, with what trigger conditions. A solo founder cannot run four tiers simultaneously. This phased playbook tells you exactly what to do in weeks, not months.

⚠

The cardinal rule of this cadence: Never move to the next phase until the current phase's exit condition is met. Not a rough "feels like it's going well" gut check — a hard measurable number. Moving phases early because of excitement destroys your ability to learn from the data. Every phase has a specific go/no-go signal below.

01

Founding Beta — Build Trust Before You Build Pricing

Month 1–3 · Validation pricing · Not the real price
Beta Price ₹899/mo

What You're Doing

  • Price: ₹899/month for beta founding members. Frame it explicitly as "30% below the launch price, permanently locked for you."
  • Tiers available: Free (30 credits) + Beta Solo only. No Firm plan. No annual billing option yet.
  • Credits: 500/month at ₹899 beta rate. Same allocation as the launch plan — just cheaper. This lets you validate credit consumption accurately.
  • Target: Recruit 25 beta users from ICAI Ernakulam chapter directly. Not through ads. In-person or WhatsApp CA community outreach only.
  • Billing: Razorpay subscriptions from Day 1. Even at ₹899, charge real money. Free users don't give real feedback.

What You're Learning

  • Credit consumption per CA profile: Article clerk vs Senior CA vs Partner — how many credits does each actually use? Is 500/month right?
  • Which feature do they use first? If it's formula Q&A, your onboarding is wrong. It should be GST reconciliation (that's where the pain is).
  • Do they hit the credit ceiling? If no one reaches 400+ credits/month, consider reducing to 400 credits at launch. If many hit 500+, either increase credits or the top-up mechanism is wrong.
  • What do they ask support about? Every support ticket is a product improvement — also your proof that the problems you're solving are real.
  • Would they recommend it? Ask 3 people directly after 4 weeks. Not NPS — just "have you told anyone about Cellix?" If yes, why. If no, why not.

Firm Plan Launch — Don't Launch It, Let It Be Asked For

Month 7–11 · ₹5,999/mo · Annual default · Pooled credits
Firm Price ₹5,999/mo

The Trigger Condition

  • Minimum 3 organic enquiries from Solo users asking "can I add my juniors?" or "we have 4 people in the firm who need this." These are your natural Firm plan buyers — they've already proven intent.
  • At least 2 enquiries from CA partners (not article clerks) — partners have purchasing authority. An article clerk asking about team plans is interesting data. A partner asking is a conversion event.
  • Pre-launch: Build the Firm plan backend (pooled credit logic, admin dashboard, per-member analytics) only after the trigger conditions above are met. Do not build ahead of demand for this tier.
  • Pricing page update: Add Firm plan to the public pricing page when you're ready to sell it. Before that, "Teams — enquire" CTA only — collects leads without product readiness pressure.
  • Annual as default: Firm plan checkout should default to annual billing. ₹59,990/year (save ₹11,998) gives you a ₹59,990 revenue event per firm sign-up. At 10 firm annual sign-ups = ₹5.99L upfront cash.

How to Convert Firm Plan Buyers

  • The ROI story for the partner: "Your 4 juniors each spend 3 hours/week on GST reconciliation and TDS matching. At ₹30,000/month salary each, that's ₹90,000/month in firm cost for manual work Cellix does in minutes. ₹5,999/month for the whole firm." Present this calculation — don't make them do the math.
  • The "better than 5 × Solo" framing: "You could pay ₹1,299 × 5 staff = ₹6,495/month with individual accounts. Or ₹5,999/month with a pooled plan where heavy months are covered automatically, you get an admin view of all usage, and client work stays separated by audit trail. Same coverage, better control, slightly lower cost."
  • Onboarding call included: For every Firm plan sign-up, personally schedule a 30-minute onboarding call. Set up their prompt library, walk through the first GST reconciliation together, show the admin dashboard. Firms that complete onboarding have drastically lower churn. This call is your best retention investment.
  • Firm plan additional seat pricing: ₹999/seat/month for seats 6–10. Above 10 seats they're talking to Enterprise. Keep this simple — don't over-engineer seat pricing until you have firms hitting the ceiling.
  • Track "firm discovery" in your CRM: For every Firm sign-up, log how they found you. Was it via a Solo user at that firm? LinkedIn? ICAI chapter? This tells you whether firm acquisition happens bottom-up (individual CA → firm) or top-down (partner decides). It likely varies by firm size.

04

Enterprise — Pure Inbound Pull, No Outbound Sales

Month 12+ · ₹25,000/mo minimum · Annual contract · Do not proactively sell
Enterprise Min ₹25,000/mo

What Enterprise Looks Like at Your Stage

  • Who this is: A CA firm with 15–50 staff, or a GCC (Global Capability Centre) finance team in Kerala needing AI-assisted Excel compliance. Not the same buyer as the ₹5,999 Firm plan partner.
  • The trigger: When a Firm plan client says "we need more than 5 seats" or "can we get a custom integration with our ERP?" — that is your enterprise signal. Respond with: "Let's talk. We have a custom arrangement for larger teams."
  • The pricing conversation: Start at ₹25,000/month minimum, annual only. Do not publish this on your pricing page — just "Contact us." The moment you publish an enterprise price, you're anchoring a negotiation you haven't had yet.
  • What you're adding vs Firm plan: Custom credit allocation (not 3,000 pooled — whatever they negotiate). Written SLA. WhatsApp support line. Custom Tally/ERP connector. Annual invoice formatted for their accounts payable (TDS-friendly). These are the four things that make ₹25,000 feel worth it vs ₹5,999.
  • Annual contract is non-negotiable: Every enterprise client must be on a 12-month contract. No month-to-month for enterprise. The commitment protects you from a single large client churning and destroying your revenue predictability.

Enterprise Sales Process (Keep It Simple)

  • Step 1 — Discovery call (30 mins): How many users? What compliance work? What's the current process? Don't pitch yet. Listen. Your goal is to understand if this is a ₹25K/month problem or a ₹50K/month problem.
  • Step 2 — Proposal (24 hours after call): One page. Custom credit allocation, price, contract term, what's included. Nothing fancy. CAs appreciate directness — a 10-page PDF with brand gradients signals "startup trying too hard."
  • Step 3 — Pilot (4 weeks, paid): Never do a free enterprise pilot. A 4-week paid pilot at ₹10,000 (non-refundable toward first contract month) filters serious buyers from tyre-kickers. Firms that won't pay for a pilot won't pay for a contract.
  • Step 4 — Contract: Annual, auto-renew with 60-day notice. Invoice quarterly (not monthly) — enterprise buyers prefer quarterly payment cycles. This also gives you 3-month revenue visibility per contract.
  • Do not hire a salesperson for this: At 2–3 enterprise clients, you (the founder) should be handling enterprise sales personally. The relationships are too early-stage and too relationship-dependent to hand off. Enterprise contracts signed by a founder carry more trust than those signed by a hired SDR.

Decision Framework

Go / No-Go Signals at Each Phase Gate

These are the hard numbers that tell you whether to advance, hold, or diagnose. Do not use "momentum" or "feeling" as a signal — use these metrics only.

Phase 1 → Phase 2: Green Light
20+ paying + 70% M2 retention
20 paying beta users AND at least 14 of the first-month cohort still active in Month 2. Both conditions must be true. Revenue: ₹17,980 MRR. If yes → launch ₹1,299 plan publicly.
Phase 1 → Phase 2: Yellow Light
15–19 users OR 60% retention
You have market signal but not enough confidence. Hold Phase 2 for 4 more weeks. Diagnose what's blocking conversion or causing churn. Is it onboarding? Features? Credit amount? Fix first, launch second.
Phase 1 → Phase 2: Red Light
<12 users after 3 months
Do not advance to Phase 2. Do not adjust pricing. The problem is not the price — it's either the product (features missing), the positioning (CAs don't see the value), or the channel (you're reaching the wrong CAs). Diagnose before spending more time on pricing.
Phase 2 → Phase 3 (Firm Plan): Green Light
3+ organic firm enquiries
Three or more Solo users have proactively asked about team access for their firm. AND you have 50+ Solo paying users. Both. This means demand for the Firm plan is pulling you, not you pushing it. Build it now.
Phase 3 → Phase 4 (Enterprise): Green Light
1 Firm plan at 80%+ credit use
At least one Firm plan client is regularly hitting 80%+ of 3,000 pooled credits AND has 5+ seats active. This means the Firm plan is a ceiling for them, not a fit. They're a natural Enterprise upgrade conversation. Start it.
Price Change Signal
30%+ annual plan take-up
If 30%+ of new Solo sign-ups choose annual billing without prompting, you have pricing power. That's the signal to test ₹1,499/mo for new users (grandfather existing at ₹1,299). Never raise prices on existing users — only on new sign-ups.
